01 —Context & Motivation

Why existing methodologies fail

Traditional management methodologies — whether Waterfall or Agile in its various forms (Scrum, Kanban, SAFe) — were designed for human execution. Their foundational assumption: work is done by people, coordination takes time, and speed is limited by human capacity.

This assumption breaks down the moment AI agents capable of the following enter the team:

  • Writing, testing and documenting a function in minutes, not hours
  • Managing a server, applying a patch and verifying infrastructure state without sleep
  • Working on dozens of tasks in parallel simultaneously
  • Producing prototypes faster than the team can convene a planning meeting
Scrum assumptions vs. reality with agents

Waterfall's concrete failure: Waterfall assumes sequential phases and stable requirements. Agents produce iteratively, in parallel and continuously — sequential phasing is a natural brake for them.

What actually changed

The shift is not just in speed. The nature of human work in a technical team is changing. Previously: People = Executors, agents assist. Now: People = Architects, agents execute.

Fundamental shift — the role of people in the agentic world

Human value shifts from implementation to: defining intent (what we want and why), architecting guardrails (what must never happen), governance (control, validation, escalation) and strategic decisions (pivots, priorities, risks).

GALDUR is the methodology that structures this new reality.

People don't move the world with their hands.
They move it with the precision of their intentions.

02 —Seven Principles of GALDUR

7 core principles of GALDUR

P1 — Intent-first

Work does not begin with a task list. It begins with a clearly defined intent — a structured document that an agent can read, interpret and execute against. Intent is not a prompt. It is a contract.

P2 — Continuous flow

No fixed cadences. Work flows continuously, governed by event-triggered governance gates, not by the calendar. The team responds to signals, not to scheduled meetings.

P3 — Layered autonomy

Every task has an explicitly assigned governance level (L0–L3). Autonomy is not a binary choice — it is a continuum calibratable by task type, reversibility, blast radius and sensitivity.

P4 — Deterministic orchestration

Agents do not decide on the order of work or on workflow sequences. A deterministic engine governs state transitions. Agents execute within their bounded scope.

P5 — Spec as living artefact

Intent Spec is not static documentation. It is a versioned, machine-readable document that evolves together with the work — agents can propose clarifications, curators approve them.

P6 — Drift as primary metric

We are not interested in velocity (how much was done). We care about drift — how much execution deviated from intent. Low drift = good specs and guardrails. High drift = specs are ambiguous or agents are crossing boundaries.

P7 — Hard Locks are inviolable

There are categories of actions an agent must never perform without explicit human approval, regardless of any instructions in the spec. These locks are hardcoded in the governance engine, not in the Intent Spec.

04 —Roles in a GALDUR Team

Role structure and responsibilities

Intent Architect (formerly: Tech Lead / Architect)

Responsibility: Defines and prioritizes Intent Specs. Decides on architecture, data models, system integrations. Approves specs before crystallization.

Typical activities: Writes or reviews Intent Specs for L2/L3 tasks. Decides on architectural pivots. Defines domain guardrails and hard locks for new domains. Leads Trace Audits.

Key shift: Does not spend time on implementation. Spends time on quality of intent and architecture of boundaries.

Governance Steward (formerly: Scrum Master / Ops Manager)

Responsibility: Manages governance gates, escalations, monitors drift metrics, handles timeout escalations.

Typical activities: Monitors Drift Dashboard. Triages agent escalations. Ensures specs meet minimum standard before activation. Manages Hard Lock incidents. Coordinates Trace Audits.

Key shift: Does not coordinate people — coordinates the interface between agents and humans.

Agent Curator (formerly: Developer / Sysadmin / DBA)

Responsibility: Reviews agent outputs, refines specs, resolves escalations. Domain expert for specific areas (code, infra, network, DB...).

Typical activities: Validates agent outputs at Validation Gates. Processes escalations requiring domain expertise. Proposes spec refinements based on outputs. Holds contextual knowledge that agents lack.

Key shift: Does not write all code or admin everything manually — is the quality gate and escalation handler.

Agents

Execute Intent Spec tasks within the assigned governance level. Log drift, escalate when boundaries are exceeded, propose spec refinements. Types: coding_agent, infra_agent, db_agent, network_agent, security_agent, docs_agent, analysis_agent.

05 —Intent Spec — Living Artifact

5.1 What is an Intent Spec

Intent Spec is the primary working artifact of the GALDUR methodology. It is not a user story (too vague), a technical specification (too detailed), a JIRA task (too atomic), nor documentation (static). It is a structured contract between human intent and the agent executing it. It has two parts:

  • Header — human-authored, strategic, describes intent
  • Execution Body — machine-readable, operational, drives execution

5.2 State lifecycle

Intent Spec lifecycle — states, transitions and who can initiate them

Agent-initiated transitions: ACTIVE → ESCALATED (boundary exceeded), ACTIVE → PENDING VALIDATION (task completion), ACTIVE → SUSPENDED (blocking dependency).

Always human-initiated transitions: DRAFT → CRYSTALLIZED, CRYSTALLIZED → ACTIVE, PENDING VALIDATION → VALIDATED or REVERTED, ESCALATED → ACTIVE.

5.3 Spec versioning

Intent Spec is versioned just like code — in a git repository. Any change to scope or constraints requires a new version and re-crystallization.

Change typeBumpWho can
Typo fix / more precise wordingMinor (v1 → v1.1)Curator or Agent (with flagging)
Scope or constraints changeMajor (v1 → v2) + re-crystallizationIntent Architect
Adding a task to execution bodyMinorCurator
Governance level change upMajor + re-crystallizationIntent Architect
Governance level change downMinorCurator

5.4 Agent-proposed refinements (Amendments)

An agent may propose spec refinements (Amendment) based on what it discovers during execution — it must not change the spec unilaterally. The agent MUST stop execution of the affected task and wait for a decision. The Curator has 24 hours to decide, then it escalates to the Intent Architect. Rejected amendment → agent continues without change and logs to drift_log.

09 —Trace Audit

9.1 What is a Trace Audit

Trace Audit is the GALDUR equivalent of a retrospective — but with a key difference: we do not ask how the team felt, we ask what the data tells us about the quality of our specs and guardrails.

Trace Audit takes place:

  • Every 2–4 weeks (or after a major milestone / incident)
  • Always after a Hard Lock incident
  • Always after Drift Score > 15 on any spec

Participants: Intent Architect + Governance Steward (mandatory), relevant Curators (based on domains in review period).

10 —Hard Locks — absolute prohibitions

Hard Locks are categories of actions that an agent must never perform without explicit real-time human approval. These locks are implemented in the governance engine — they are not part of the Intent Spec, agents cannot override them, curators cannot delegate them.

🔴 10.1 Universal Hard Locks — apply to all domains, without exception

CategorySpecific prohibitions
Credentials & SecretsHandling private keys/passwords/API keys in plain-text · Writing secrets to logs or DB without encryption · Rotating prod credentials without rollback plan
Destructive DB operationsDELETE without WHERE on production DB · DROP TABLE/DATABASE in production · TRUNCATE prod tables · Overwriting production backups
Identity & AccessAny IAM/permissions change without human approval · Creating or deleting admin/root account · SSH key in authorized_keys on prod server
Critical infrastructureChange to core routing tables or BGP · Change to prod firewall rules (allow or deny) · Shutdown/restart of critical prod server without change window
External communicationEmail/notification to >10 recipients · Webhook to external system with PII · Communication implying organizational commitment
Payments & ComplianceHandling payment logic or billing data · Exporting PII without GDPR audit trail · Deleting data subject to retention policy

10.2 Domain-specific Hard Locks

🔴 INFRASTRUCTURE:
  ├── Restore from backup to production
  ├── Server decommission without data backup verification
  └── Change to production SSL/TLS configuration without downtime coordination

🔴 DATABASE:
  ├── Backup restore to production DB
  ├── Any UPDATE without WHERE on prod table > 10K rows
  └── Change to DB replication configuration

🔴 NETWORK:
  ├── Change or deletion of DNS records for prod domains (A, MX, NS)
  ├── Change to core switch configuration
  └── Any VPN configuration change affecting prod traffic

🔴 SECURITY:
  ├── Incident response containment (system isolation, IP blocking)
  ├── Deletion or modification of audit logs
  └── Any exploit attempt or penetration (even authorized pen test)

10.3 What happens when a Hard Lock is detected

  1. Agent immediately stops execution
  2. Spec transitions to ESCALATED:hard_lock state
  3. Governance Steward receives immediate notification (not async Drift Signal)
  4. Incident is written to a separate Hard Lock Incident Log
  5. Spec CANNOT continue without explicit Intent Architect approval
  6. Hard Lock incident is ALWAYS reviewed at the next Trace Audit

11 —Adapting to Team Size

Solo developer with agents

Intent Architect = Governance Steward = Agent Curator = YOU

Adjustments: L2 tasks — you give consent yourself before execution (but still before, not after). Trace Audit: 1x monthly, personal review of drift logs. Drift Dashboard: terminal output / simple CLI. Hard Locks apply unchanged.

Small team (2–4 people)

1 Intent Architect (senior / tech lead) · 1–2 Agent Curators (full-stack, ops) · Governance Steward rotates or is a shared role

Adjustments: Any Curator can validate L1 tasks. L2 tasks require Intent Architect approval. Weekly Spec Backlog Review (30 min) replaces sprint planning.

Medium team (5–15 people)

1–2 Intent Architects (per domain: Dev IA + Ops IA) · 1 Governance Steward (dedicated role) · 3–10 Agent Curators (specialized per domain)

Recommendations: Separate spec backlog per domain. Domain-specific Trace Audits (Dev, Ops) + cross-domain quarterly. Governance Steward has access to all drift dashboards.

ADDENDUM I —GALDUR @ Scale — comparison with SAFe

S1. Why SAFe was created and what it solves

SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework, Dean Leffingwell, 2011) addresses three fundamental problems of scaling Agile: Alignment (how to ensure dozens of teams pull in the same direction), Dependencies (how to synchronize work between teams), and Strategic Continuity (how to translate business strategy into the work of hundreds of developers).

SAFe responds with a four-level hierarchy: Portfolio → Large Solution → Program (ART) → Team. The key synchronization mechanism is PI Planning — a two-day mass event where 50–125 people plan 8–12 weeks ahead.

S2. Critique of SAFe — where it fails

Ceremony overhead is enormous. Organizations describe a state where 20 people work full-time just on PI planning. PI Planning requires coordination, facilitation, preparation, and follow-up that often takes longer than the actual 2-day event.

Illusion of agility, reality of waterfall. SAFe is criticized for giving organizations the illusion of Agile adoption while preserving the original processes. Long-term PI planning (8–12 weeks ahead) is structurally very close to waterfall. Marty Cagan of the Silicon Valley Product Group stated that he does not know any strong product company using SAFe.

Top-down command and control. Teams are forced into ways of working, cannot make their own decisions, and work is assigned to them without regard for their technical knowledge. This undermines exactly the autonomy that Agile promised.

Does not address AI agents at all. SAFe was designed for human teams. In 2025–26, as agents take over large portions of execution, SAFe has no model for governing work happening in agentic workflows.

GALDUR vs. SAFe — 9 key comparisons

S4. How GALDUR scales — layer model

The key insight for scaling: in GALDUR, coordination overhead does not grow linearly with headcount, because coordination happens through specs, not meetings. The better specs are written, the less coordination people need to do actively.

Organizational layers of GALDUR @ Enterprise

S6. Synchronization without PI Planning

SAFe PI Planning addresses three things: alignment, dependency mapping and commitment. GALDUR addresses each of these differently.

Instead of Alignment: Domain Intent Broadcast — a structured YAML document issued quarterly by the Domain Intent Architect. Contains priorities, capacity guidance, cross-domain dependencies and deprioritized items. Time required: 2–4 hours of preparation, async distribution. Vs. 2 days of PI Planning.

Instead of Dependency Mapping: Dependencies are explicitly encoded in Intent Specs through the depends_on and blocks fields. The governance engine automatically blocks spec activation if its dependencies are not met, and generates a Dependency Dashboard in real time.

Instead of Commitment: SAFe creates commitment through social pressure at PI Planning. GALDUR replaces it with structural accountability — every spec has an explicit owner, every drift is logged, Trace Audit data is shared at domain and portfolio level.

S9. Advantages of GALDUR over SAFe

  • Drastically lower ceremony overhead — ~150 vs. ~900 person-hours per quarter
  • Native AI agent support — first-class citizens with governance levels from the ground up
  • Covers the entire technical organization — Dev + Ops + Infra + Security + Robots
  • Organic adoption — starts with one Crew, not a big-bang rollout
  • Specs as communication medium — context is in the artifact, not in people's heads
  • Governance is distributed — in specs and the engine, not centralized in roles
  • Product-led culture — Intent-first naturally supports outcome thinking

S10. Disadvantages and risks of GALDUR at scale

  • Spec writing is a hard discipline — the entire methodology stands or falls on spec quality; a bad spec will cause visible drift without meeting compensation
  • Non-existent tooling ecosystem — SAFe has Jira Align, certifications, consultants; GALDUR has nothing yet
  • Difficult to justify to traditional management — lacks brand recognition and a certification program
  • Spec backlog can explode — without WIP discipline, hundreds of Suspended/Draft specs accumulate; requires an explicit spec hygiene policy
  • AI governance is new territory — fintech/healthcare regulations with agents (DORA, GDPR) is an emerging discipline

S11. Migration strategy from SAFe to GALDUR

Phase 1 (months 1–3): Pilot Crew — Choose one team, dissatisfied with SAFe ceremony overhead, technically strong. Run in parallel with SAFe — still fulfills SAFe obligations, but internally works the GALDUR way. Metrics: ceremony overhead, drift rate, time from intent to validated feature.

Phase 2 (months 3–9): Domain pilot — Expand to the entire Intent Domain. Adds the Domain Intent Architect role, cross-Crew dependency management, Domain Trace Audits. At portfolio level still SAFe rhythm — inside the domain GALDUR.

Phase 3 (months 9–18): Portfolio transition — Governance Council replaces ARTs, Strategic Intent Map replaces Portfolio Kanban, PI Planning formally ended.

ADDENDUM II/R —Robotic Dimension — Physical AI

R1. Why robots belong in GALDUR

The description of GALDUR so far covers digital agents. But the methodology must also address physical agents — robots that perform work in the real world. As physical AI systems mature, organizations are deploying heterogeneous robot fleets from multiple vendors, each with proprietary protocols. Early proof-of-concept results from robotic initiatives show up to 50% reduction in unplanned downtime and 25% improvement in productivity.

The physical world adds dimensions that the digital world does not have: bodily presence, safety, irreversibility of physical actions and coexistence with humans in space.

Taxonomy of physical agents in GALDUR — 5 levels

R3. Physical Hard Locks — absolute categories

Physical Hard Locks supersede all others — they cannot be overridden in software. They are implemented in the robot's hardware as well as the orchestration software:

  • Robot must not move if a person is detected in the defined safety zone without explicit override
  • Force limit must not be exceeded (hardware limit)
  • Emergency stop must always be functional and accessible
  • Loss of communication = immediate safe stop (fail-safe)
  • Handling biological/chemical/radioactive materials requires L3 + certified protocol
  • Reconfiguration of safety parameters: always L3 + audit record
  • Physical robot must not restrict a person's movement or exit from the space (anti-trapping)

R7. Digital twin as an interface between intent and the physical world

Digital twin enables simulation before execution — the robot "plays out" the Intent Spec in a virtual environment, the governance engine validates the result before launching in the real world. In the GALDUR model: a physical Intent Spec has a mandatory field digital_twin_simulation: required | optional | n/a. For L2+ tasks of physical agents, simulation is mandatory before approval.

ADDENDUM II/H —Human Dimension

H1. The problem methodologies ignore

GALDUR so far has described roles as functional entities. But people are not roles. People are beings with social needs, emotions, ambitions, fear of relevance, need for recognition, friendly bonds and individual productivity rhythms. A methodology that ignores this will end up like SAFe — technically correct, humanly unbearable.

The research is clear: a moderate level of automation maintains sufficient worker engagement in the decision-making process, thereby continuously supporting their autonomy and competencies. Empirical research in the psychology of automation suggests that maintaining active worker involvement, even partial, helps sustain self-determined motivation, reduces feelings of monotony and prevents automation-induced disengagement. And at the same time: Human-AI teams show lower cohesion and identification than purely human teams.

Five human needs and GALDUR's structural responses

H2. Five human needs — GALDUR's structural responses

Autonomy — I have influence over my work

The greatest risk of the agentic world: a human becomes a passive validator of outputs they had no influence over. GALDUR responds: Intent Spec is written by a human (not an agent), Curator can propose alternative decompositions and different governance levels, no agent may initiate work without a crystallized spec.

Competence — I am good at what I do

The threat: agents produce outputs faster and sometimes at better quality than a human, which can erode the sense of professional value. GALDUR responds: Curator is explicitly the domain expert who evaluates agent output from a position of knowledge the agent lacks (business context, organizational memory, relationship intelligence). Trace Audits value human pattern recognition. The Intent Architect role is creative and strategic.

Belonging — I belong somewhere

GALDUR introduces structural social mechanisms: Trace Audit as a social event (last 15 minutes is open floor with no agenda) · Intent Crystallization Session (creative act without agents) · Weekly Human Sync (30 min, NOT about specs, NOT about agents — about people) · Domain Day quarterly (full day for people: demos, learnings, informal time).

Meaning — what I do has meaning

Each Curator is assigned a domain responsibility — is the guardian of quality for the entire domain. Intent Spec explicitly contains a intent section describing why — everyone working on the spec knows the bigger picture. Portfolio Intent Map is publicly visible to all team members.

Fairness — rules apply to everyone, my contribution is visible

Drift log is transparent — we see where a spec failed, not where a person failed. Hard Locks apply to all agents, all domains. Trace Audit metrics are shared. Every decision has a trace in the Amendment process.

H3. Psychological safety in GALDUR context

In Human-AI teams there is a risk: a team member knows the agent output is wrong, but feels pressure to approve it because "AI did it quickly". GALDUR guarantees:

  • Curator has veto without justification — "something doesn't seem right to me" is a legitimate reason for revision; an agent never argues back
  • Escalation is rewarded — Trace Audit explicitly tracks "escalations that prevented incidents" as positive metrics
  • Drift log is blameless — "curator X has a high rejection rate" = "specs in domain X need refinement", not an evaluation of the person
  • Hard Lock incident is not a scandal — postmortem is about improving the system, not finding someone to blame

H5. Onboarding into a GALDUR team

Week 1: Read the 10 most recent validated Intent Specs in the domain. Attend a Trace Audit as an observer. Do not validate anything — just watch and ask questions.

Week 2: Shadow a senior Curator during 5 Validation Gates. Discuss every decision: why approve, why reject. Write down your own observations.

Week 3: Supervised validation — validate yourself, but consult every decision with the senior. Propose 3 things that could be improved in the specs.

Week 4: Independent work for L0–L1 tasks. Mentor available for consultation.

Onboarding output: The new member understands three things: what a good spec is, how to recognize quality agent output, when to escalate. And one human thing: they know who to turn to.

H6. "Moral crumple zones" — who bears responsibility?

In Human-AI systems, responsibility for an AI error is transferred to the nearest human in the hierarchy — even if the actual decision was made by an agent. In GALDUR context: the curator approved the specification, the agent performed a destructive operation, the curator bears responsibility.

GALDUR responds: Responsibility is shared, not transferred — Curator is responsible for the quality of the Validation Gate, Intent Architect for the quality of specs and constraints. The audit trail documents decisions, not culprits. Hard Locks protect not only the system but also the Curator — an agent will never perform an action for which the Curator would bear disproportionate responsibility. Blameless postmortem is mandatory: "where did the system fail?" not "who failed?"

ADDENDUM II/F —Financial Dimension

F1. The core tension: agent time vs. linear financial time

Agents work asynchronously, in parallel, in burst modes. Finance and management live in linearly flowing time — quarters, fiscal year, investor milestones, vendor payment cycles. This tension is real. 63% of finance teams have problems with forecasts beyond a six-month horizon when using traditional methods.

Three-horizon financial model of GALDUR

F3. Intent-based milestones — not calendar-based

Traditional milestones are calendar-based ("by March 31 we will deliver feature X"). GALDUR introduces Intent-based milestones — a milestone is met when all linked specs are validated, not when a date passes. Confidence level is automatically calculated from drift metrics of active specs (weighted_spec_completion * (1 - avg_drift_score)).

F4. Per-Spec Cost Model

Every Intent Spec has an assigned financial dimension: human_hours, agent_compute_units, infra_cost and total_estimated. The governance engine continuously tracks actual vs. estimated. This way the portfolio layer can see in real time where money goes — without time tracking, project reporting or manual controlling.

F5. Rolling Forecast instead of annual budget

GALDUR naturally implements the rolling forecast approach: initiatives are funded in stages — each with clear continuation criteria tied to results. This makes it easier to stop or redirect work without every cancellation being perceived as failure.

F6. Agent Compute Budget — a new accounting category

Traditional accounting: salaries, hardware, software licenses, cloud. An agentic organization needs a new category: agent compute budget. Every spec has a max_agent_budget (hard cap) — the governance engine stops the agent upon reaching 80% of budget and escalates (throttling alert) before the limit is exceeded.

F7. Business Value Framework per spec

Every Intent Spec can (for F2+, recommended) contain: value_type (revenue_enabling / cost_reduction / risk_mitigation / compliance / capability_building), estimated_annual_value_eur, value_realization_date and measurement_method. This enables ROI calculation per spec: (Business Value − Total Cost) / Total Cost × 100.

F10. Potentially transformative concepts (not yet widespread)

Outcome-based agent pricing — payment for validated result, not for tokens and compute. Specialized agent firms are beginning to offer this experimentally — a natural extension of the GALDUR cost model.

Agent capacity as a tradeable asset — an organization with mature agent governance and good Intent Specs has assets that can be leased (Crew-as-a-service), sold as "validated agent workflow", or invested in a joint venture.

Financial digital twin — a virtual model of the entire financial behavior of the organization, updated in real time from the Drift Dashboard, spec completion data and cost tracking. CFO can run "what if" analyses: "what will happen to Q3 milestones if I shift 30% of capacity from domain A to domain B?"

Kitchen as a model of the GALDUR team

ADDENDUM IV —Knowledge Domains — Sales, Marketing, Legal, Finance

Why knowledge work needs governance just like IT

The decision is human. The execution is agentic.
Governance is the bridge between them.
The more precise the intent, the better the result.

GALDUR in its base form covers Dev and Ops domains. But in a hybrid company — where agents work across the entire organization — the same principles fully apply to sales, marketing, legal and finance. The risks are different, but the structure of the problem is identical:

  • Agent produces outputs quickly — but quality and approval depend on humans
  • Some actions are irreversible — a sent customer email, a signed contract, a published post
  • The blast radius of an uncontrolled action can be enormous — legal commitment, reputational damage, regulatory issue
  • Without explicit governance, agent autonomy becomes a risk, not an advantage

Key insight: knowledge work has a higher share of L2–L3 tasks than IT. The reason is that knowledge work outputs are directly visible to customers, partners or regulators — and a mistake cannot be "rolled back" like a database migration.

Extended GALDUR coverage domains — hybrid company

How roles change in non-tech context

GALDUR roles remain — only their specific content changes. The Intent Architect does not need to be a technical person. The Curator does not need to know code. What remains the same: humans hold intent, agents execute, governance decides the degree of autonomy.
